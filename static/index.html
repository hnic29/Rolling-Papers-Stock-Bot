Overview

This bot runs a rules-based version of the small-account momentum strategy popularized by day trader Ross Cameron (Warrior Trading): find a stock that's already moving hard on real volume, wait for it to pull back and hold, then buy the moment it breaks back to a new high — with a hard stop below the pullback and no fixed ceiling on the upside. It is not a "buy good companies" strategy. It deliberately hunts small, thinly-floated, volatile stocks having an unusually active day, because those are the ones capable of the fast, large percentage moves this style of trading targets.

Every rule described on this page is enforced in code, not just intent — app/strategies/small_account_pullback.py is the actual decision engine, used identically by manual "Run Tick", full automation, and the backtester.

The Universe — What Stocks It Even Looks At

Every scan cycle draws candidates from four sources and merges them:

  • A real-time gap lane — live snapshot prices (zero data lag) across the whole market, computing each stock's gap off yesterday's close the moment it moves. This is what makes the bot able to act from the opening bell instead of waiting ~16 minutes for consolidated data; gap-lane candidates carry their live numbers straight into entry evaluation.
  • A true whole-market sweep — every tradable NASDAQ/NYSE/AMEX common stock (~8,000+, refreshed daily) screened each cycle against the actual pillar math on consolidated data: price $2–$20, up 10%+, 1M+ shares traded, 5x relative volume. Nothing needs to be on any list in advance to be found.
  • Today's live top gainers — Alpaca's movers screener as a fast secondary net (and the fallback if a sweep fails mid-cycle), filtered the same way.
  • A screened watchlist (data/stock_universe.txt) — pre-screened small-floats worth watching even on days they haven't yet moved enough to surface in the sweep.

The float ceiling matters most: a stock with billions of shares outstanding (think AAPL or TSLA) mathematically cannot move the way this strategy needs, no matter how good the rest of the logic is. There is deliberately no float floor — a 1M minimum once existed and excluded XPON (933K float) the day it ran +80% on 86M shares, and JUNS (478K float) the day it gapped +60% on 52M shares. The volume pillars already guarantee tradeability on the only day the bot would touch a name.

The watchlist is a snapshot — it only updates when scripts/build_universe.py is re-run, and the bot flags its age in the status message once it's 45+ days old. The top-gainers feed is live every cycle and needs no maintenance.

The Five Pillars — Scoring a Candidate

Every symbol in the universe gets scanned and scored against five independent checks. A stock needs at least 4 of 5 to be considered a candidate at all — anything less is marked hold and never evaluated further.

  1. Relative volume ≥ 5x — today's consolidated volume-so-far vs. a same-time-of-day-adjusted average of the last 20 trading days. (Comparing partial-day volume against a naive full-day average would understate this all morning — the bot prorates for exactly that reason.)
  2. Total volume ≥ 1,000,000 shares — enough real activity to actually get filled.
  3. Up at least 10% on the day — a real move, not noise.
  4. Price between $2 and $20 — the "small account" sweet spot; cheap enough to size meaningfully, not so cheap it's a penny-stock trap.
  5. Float at or below 20,000,000 shares — genuinely small-float, the structural reason it can move fast. Tightens to 10,000,000 automatically on a "cold" cycle (fewer than 2 candidates clearing 4-of-5 market-wide) and relaxes back once genuine setups reappear — Warrior Trading's own trading-plan template ties float tolerance to market regime the same way.

News catalyst and "hot sector" (AI, biotech, China/Chinese tech, tech) are also surfaced as reasons in the scanner, but they're informational only — they never add to the score.

Reading the Catalyst — Not Just "Has News"

Every headline the scanner sees gets classified, not just flagged true/false. This exists because of a real, expensive lesson: Ross Cameron's 2026-08-25 GRML trade lost $8,000 on a stock whose catalyst was a rare-earth acquisition plus a proposed public offering — and an offering announcement is one of the most reliable "day trader beware" signals in the small-cap world. It means dilution is coming, and informed sellers often sell straight into that exact headline, which is precisely the fast reversal GRML showed.

  • Positive catalysts — contract/purchase order, FDA/clinical trial result, acquisition or merger, earnings or a record-results operational update, strategic partnership, patent grant, analyst upgrade.
  • Dilution-risk catalysts — a proposed/registered/private-placement stock offering, a reverse stock split, a going-concern warning, a delisting notice, a financial restatement. These don't block a trade outright (the pattern can still run hard first, and the stop/2R/giveback layers still apply once in) — but position size is automatically halved, and you get a phone push saying exactly why.
  • Unclassified — has news, but not a pattern recognized yet; still shown, just not weighted either way.

Keyword-based, not machine learning, on purpose — every classification traces back to the exact phrase that triggered it, visible right in the scanner's reasons. Applies everywhere news is checked: the lagged whole-market sweep and the real-time gap lane both classify catalysts now (the gap lane never checked news at all before this).

The Entry — First Pullback, New High

Clearing 4-of-5 only makes a stock a candidate. To actually trigger a buy, its most recent 1-minute candles must also pass every one of these — a single failure anywhere returns hold, not a partial trade:

  • The pullback retraced 50% or less of the prior move up (a deeper retrace means the move is probably over)
  • The proposed entry price is above the 9-period EMA (still in the short-term uptrend)
  • The pullback candle closed above VWAP (buyers are still in control on average)
  • MACD is positive — skipped for the first ~26 minutes of the session, before it's meaningfully warmed up, rather than treating an undefined reading as a rejection
  • The most recent candle just broke to a new high — this is the actual trigger; everything else just qualifies the setup
  • Volume on the pullback is lighter than volume on the push up (healthy, low-conviction selling, not a real reversal)

The stop-loss is set just below the pullback's low. Risk per share is entry minus stop — this number drives position sizing below.

Position Sizing — How Many Shares

Every buy is sized by the smallest of three independent caps, all computed live off your actual trading bankroll (never a fixed dollar figure — see the Bankroll tab for what that means):

Risk cap
2% of bankroll ÷ risk-per-share
Position cap
20% of bankroll ÷ entry price
Bankroll cap
whatever's actually still available to trade ÷ entry price

Whichever of the three produces the fewest shares wins. If that's zero, the trade is skipped entirely — no partial/rounding-up trades.

The Exit — Stop-Loss Always, No Fixed Ceiling

Every auto-entered position rests one order at the broker: the stop-loss. There is deliberately no automatic take-profit. Ross Cameron's own philosophy is to hold a genuine winner past the first visible resistance level rather than cap it there — a fixed take-profit would work against that on purpose.

Instead, every open position gets three layers of management, every cycle:

  • Exit indicators — a topping-tail candle (long upper wick: buyers pushed, sellers rejected hard), a red close, MACD crossing below its own signal line, or the latest candle's volume dropping well under the recent pace all fire an immediate market exit. (Level-two signals — large/hidden sellers, red-tape bursts — are supported in the code but not live-wired; see Limitations.)
  • Breakeven protection at 2R — the first time a position is up twice its risk (Ross's minimum-target multiple), the entry stop is replaced with a stop at the entry price: "move the stop up toward break-even." A proven winner can no longer become a loss.
  • Giveback exit — once 2R has printed, falling back below +1R closes the position and banks what's left, instead of riding the whole gain back down waiting for a reversal candle.

So a winner can still run indefinitely — but past 2R its worst case is breakeven, past 2R-then-fade its worst case is roughly +1R, and the moment real deterioration shows, it's out.

This monitoring runs on every open position, every cycle, regardless of how it was opened (auto or manual) and regardless of whether Auto-Trading is currently switched on — see Bot States.

Daily Discipline — When It Stops For The Day

Three separate rules can pause new entries for the rest of the trading day. Any one of them tripping sets a walked_away_for_day flag that only clears at the next trading day:

  • 3 losing trades in a row
  • Giving back 50%+ of the day's peak realized profit (locks in that you had a good day, even if it's fading)
  • 60 minutes with no trade taken while auto-trading is on ("momentum has cooled")

Walking away only pauses new risk. Existing open positions are still watched and protected the entire time — see the exit logic above. This state, along with today's trade count and P&L, survives a server restart; it doesn't quietly reset if the app crashes or gets redeployed mid-session.

The Automation Cycle — Every Minute, In Order

While the bot process is running, this sequence repeats every 60 seconds, every step independent of the others failing:

  1. Sync — reconcile every pending order and open exit against Alpaca's real fill data. This runs headless, with or without the dashboard open.
  2. Notify on open/close — a phone push exactly once when the regular session starts (9:30 AM ET) and once when it ends (4:00 PM ET, with a same-day P&L recap). Runs every day automatically, independent of Auto-Trading being on.
  3. Manage open positions — check every held position for an exit indicator, close if one fired. Runs unconditionally, even with Auto-Trading off.
  4. Stop here if Auto-Trading is off. Nothing below this line runs.
  5. Check the market is actually open (Alpaca's own clock) OR the premarket window (7:00–9:30 AM ET by default)
  6. Check today's walk-away state — new entries pause if already tripped
  7. Check the trading window (9:30 AM – 4:00 PM ET, skipped during premarket)
  8. Check the bankroll has anything available to trade
  9. Scan the universe, score every symbol, rank by score
  10. For each qualifying candidate not already held, run the full entry pattern check and submit a sized buy if it triggers — a market order with a bracket stop during regular hours, or a limit order with no bracket during premarket (Alpaca disallows both outside 9:30–16:00). Premarket additionally requires a real, non-dilution-risk news catalyst — no exceptions on "leading gainer" or "hot sector" the way regular hours allows.

A premarket fill holds no broker-side stop until regular hours opens and one gets armed automatically — exit-indicator monitoring (step 2) is its only protection until then. This exists because Ross Cameron's entire 2026-08-25 P&L happened 7:00–9:30 AM, fully resolved before a regular-hours-only bot would ever look. Requiring a genuine catalyst specifically here comes straight from Warrior Trading's own trading-plan template, which lists it as a premarket-only requirement — with no resting stop possible, a real story behind the move matters more here than anywhere else in the system.

Honest Limitations

  • Level-two order flow isn't live-wired. The strategy code supports it (large seller, hidden seller, red-tape burst, buying slowing), but nothing currently feeds it real data — those specific exit signals never fire in practice today. The candle-based exits (topping tail, red close) do the real work.
  • Float data is best-effort, not guaranteed: near-qualifying symbols get a live lookup each scan (FMP first, Yahoo Finance as fallback, each cached for a day), with the local backfilled list as the last resort. If every source fails, the float pillar simply doesn't score that cycle — a 4-of-5 candidate still qualifies.
  • The universe list only updates when manually re-run. The 45-day staleness warning tells you when, but nothing does it automatically yet.
  • Backtesting has real gaps too: historical news isn't available for past dates, so that one of the five pillars never scores in a backtest; float scores from your local metadata list (today's value, not a historical snapshot). And Alpaca's free data feed typically only goes back to around 2020.
